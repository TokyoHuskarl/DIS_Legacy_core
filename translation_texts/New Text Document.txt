Faction: The Empire
The Altippia Empire
Also referred to as the Kyugo Empire when referring specifically to its western territories, the Altippia Empire was a great empire that once dominated the entire Altippian continent in ancient times. It originated from the union of the human oligarchy known as the "Kyugo Republic" and the demon-led "Great Kulsar Kingdom," both of which had coexisted under the "Altippia Grand Alliance."

This alliance transformed into an empire when Gaius the Gray, a human, was crowned as the "Supreme Emperor." Under the banners of the famed human legions, the empire conquered everything within the known world—from the tundras in the north to the deserts in the south. Altippia was a multi-ethnic military state that expanded its territories through conquest, assimilating various races into its structure.

Although the empire boasted an advanced magical civilization, much of its technological legacy remains unexplained even in modern times. In fact, most of the empire’s magical knowledge was lost during the Dark Ages following its collapse, with many spells and artifacts now existing only in legend.

Governance and Social Structure
The empire’s governance was characterized by an inclusive civic law system that granted legal equality to all free citizens, regardless of their species or ethnicity. This policy allowed for the upper classes of conquered regions to integrate, and the empire’s dominance, built on cultural assimilation, remained extensive and long-lasting.

Later generations often idealized the empire as a symbol of coexistence and prosperity among different species, particularly in contrast to the interspecies conflicts that emerged after its fall.

However, recent studies suggest that internal social conflicts were deeply embedded within the empire. These tensions arose from cultural frictions between the patriarchal Kyugo civic order and the matriarchal traditions of the demon clans descending from the Demon King Kuu. Furthermore, despite the empire’s apparent inclusivity, an undeniable divide remained between the ruling Kyugo humans, the upper-class demons, and other subject races. The challenge of governing vast, culturally and physically diverse populations under a single imperial authority was a monumental and ultimately unsolvable dilemma, even for the most powerful empire in recorded history.

The foundation of imperial society was built upon legal structures that were inherently human-centric. While citizenship technically provided equal legal standing to all, the empire was, at its core, still a "human empire." Laws were drafted by humans, and the Senate, which was initially composed of representatives from various races, eventually became a mere advisory body serving the human emperor—the "First Citizen of the Empire."

The Empire’s Decline
Despite its internal contradictions, the empire successfully maintained control over the ancient world until the onset of the so-called "Demon Cataclysm," which marked the beginning of its downfall. The economic devastation that followed led to the empire’s increasing reliance on heavy taxation, which only accelerated its decline.

The true nature of the Demon Cataclysm remains unknown. While earlier historians speculated that it might have been a large-scale volcanic eruption, later geological studies refuted this theory. With no other widely accepted explanations, the event remains shrouded in mystery.

In any case, the cataclysm triggered a dramatic cooling period that lasted for centuries, devastating the empire’s agriculture. Many northern plantations collapsed, leading to severe food shortages that turned imperial streets into mass graves filled with the starved and dying. Eventually, magical advancements in agriculture helped alleviate the crisis by the late 6th century, but by then, the empire had already lost its former prosperity.

As food production in the south remained stable, the empire’s lifeline shifted towards these regions, and military resources were increasingly concentrated there.

The extreme climate change and the ensuing hardship led many in the empire to interpret the crisis as "the Demon’s Curse," fueling apocalyptic cults. Among them, the Church of Canael became particularly influential—a topic that will not be explored further in this text.

The Empire’s True Nature
It is true that the empire brought prosperity to many regions. Under "Pax Imperia," interspecies conflicts were largely suppressed for generations. For those who lived during the imperial era, "the light of civilization" was synonymous with "the lights of imperial cities."

However, this prosperity was built upon systematic exploitation. The empire’s economy relied heavily on war slaves, and its survival depended on continuous expansion through military conquest. To maintain the "Altippia Empire," the empire was forced to wage endless wars against external enemies.

Ultimately, when the empire had subjugated all external threats, it faced a contradiction it could not solve through military might:
"Now that we have taken everything from everyone, from whom shall we take next?"

The Fall of the Empire
Following a series of brutal civil wars, the empire split into eastern and western halves. The western empire, which remained a human-led state, succumbed to financial collapse, plagues, and foreign invasions. Meanwhile, the eastern empire, which became a demon-led state, struggled under mounting external pressures and eventually regressed into a feudal society led by a Demon King under the title of Basileus (King of Kings).

With the empire’s fall, its legal and administrative order collapsed entirely. Various successor states—formed along racial and ethnic lines—rose and fragmented into competing kingdoms. The colossus that once ruled the known world had fallen, giving way to the medieval Altippia, where the human Ozark Kingdom and the demon-dominated Demon Kingdom became the two dominant powers.

Author’s Notes
Unsurprisingly, the empire is heavily inspired by the Roman Empire. However, since the author is Japanese, elements of the Chinese Empire may have influenced its depiction as well. In later expansions of Legacy, the portrayal of the empire also incorporates elements of modern Western superpowers.

Although it is sometimes called the Kyugo Empire in related works, this term is a human-centric perspective—the proper name remains "Altippia Empire." The reason "Altippia Empire" is rarely mentioned in the story is that, within the narrative's time period, no other empire exists in recorded history. While it is possible that major powers exist outside the known world, the empire is unquestionably the dominant force of its time.

The empire is often described as excessively reliant on military force—this is intentional, reflecting its role as the quintessential villainous faction in the game.

While its social sciences remain at a classical level (e.g., slavery remains integral to its economy), the empire still surpasses modern-day technology in many ways, especially in military advancements. In many respects, the empire resembles a Civilization game where a player focused solely on military tech, achieved a domination victory, and then suffered an economic collapse.

Final Reflection
To those who lived through its era—and even to those who came after—the empire was an awe-inspiring colossus.

In earlier drafts, the author jokingly described the empire as a "foolish nation that expanded recklessly until its economy exploded." However, after years of reflection, a different perspective emerged.

While the empire’s structure was deeply flawed, its rule did bring relative peace. After its collapse, the world was plunged into a state of endless ethnic and racial conflicts—a cycle of hatred that continued indefinitely.

Even though the empire's contradictions inevitably led to its fall, the sheer fact that it maintained order for centuries—despite its internal divisions—was an astonishing feat. Countless individuals must have dedicated themselves to upholding "Pax Imperia," striving to preserve a better world for future generations.

With the fall of the Altippia Empire, the world of this setting may have already reached its ultimate decline. What follows is nothing more than the endless repetition of medieval fantasy tropes—until the end of days.
